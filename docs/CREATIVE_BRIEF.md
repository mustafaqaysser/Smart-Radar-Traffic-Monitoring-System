# Creative brief

Three genuinely different directions were developed before committing to one. Each has its own cuisine, city,
tone and visual world. The chosen direction — **Zill / ظل** — is documented in full in `docs/BRAND.md`.

---

## Direction A — **Zill · ظل** (chosen)

**Cuisine & concept.** Cooking of the Arabian Peninsula — Hijazi, Najdi and Red Sea — organised by the hours of the
day. A courtyard restaurant that follows the shade across its floor from first coffee to midnight: every part of the
day has its own menu, its own light and its own corner of the house.

**City.** Jeddah first — a coral-stone house in Al-Balad, the old port town — and a second house in Riyadh, a
mud-brick courtyard on the edge of the palm groves of Wadi Hanifah. Both cities run on the same clock (Asia/Riyadh)
but not the same sun: Jeddah's sunset comes some twenty minutes after Riyadh's, and Jeddah, south of the Tropic of
Cancer, loses its noon shadow completely twice a year.

**Name.** *Zill* (ظل) — shade, shadow. One syllable, two Arabic letters, effortless in both languages. In Arabic,
*khafif al-zill* ("light of shade") also means charming, easy company — the kind of host we want to be.
Clash check (web search, 2026-09-29): *Zur Zill* (a regional German restaurant in Ulm) and *Zill's Tunnel* (Leipzig)
are small, unrelated businesses in another market; *Zill al-Qamar* («ظل القمر», "Moonlight") in Riyadh carries a
different, longer name. No well-known restaurant called Zill / ظل exists in the Arab world.

**Positioning.** Premium-casual, all day. Breakfast for families on a Friday, a long working lunch, coffee and sweets in
the afternoon, a candle-lit dinner, and a seven-course tasting menu — *The Sundial* — for the nights that matter.
Hospitality measured in shade: the first thing a host in this land has always offered.

**Story.** In a hot country, shade is the first courtesy. Before coffee, before dates, a host would move you out of
the sun. The old houses of Jeddah and Najd were built around a courtyard, and the day moved around it with the
shade — bread and coffee by the east wall, the long lunch under the trellis, the quiet hour in the deepest room, and at
night the whole courtyard, open to the sky. Zill is built on that rhythm and cooks to it.

**Concept line.** **"Every hour has its shade."** — «لكلّ ساعةٍ ظلُّها»
Cadenced like an Arabic proverb (*li-kulli maqāmin maqāl*), it justifies every decision: menus by the hour, a palette
that follows the sun, shadows that move, reservations as "book your shade" («احجز ظلّك»).

**Palette.** Sunlit plaster, coral stone and mud in the light; the cool violet-brown of real shadow on a warm wall;
saffron for the sun itself; henna and palm green borrowed from painted Najdi doors; a deep indigo night lit by lamps.
Warm light, cool shade — physically correct, and nowhere near gold-on-black.

**Type pairing.** Reem Kufi (Arabic display — a geometric Kufi whose tall verticals stand like sundial gnomons) with
Imbue (Latin display — a condensed, high-contrast face: thick stroke as shade, hairline as light). Markazi Text for
running text in both scripts, designed as one bilingual family. IBM Plex Mono for the "instrument" layer: time,
coordinates and the sun's angle.

**Photography.** Hard, low sun; long, crisp shadows; warm highlights and cool shade; plaster, stone, clay, palm wood and
linen. Food at 45° and top-down on stoneware, hands in frame, no faces, no text. Night frames lit by warm lamps
against blue ambient.

**Motion language.** Shade moves slowly and never jumps. Long decelerations, sweeping wipes that travel along the
current shadow angle, reveals like shade sliding off a wall. No bounce except the smallest tactile feedback.

**Signature moments.**
1. **The Gnomon** — a first-visit preloader: the ظ monogram stands as a sundial; its shadow sweeps from dawn to the
   sun's real current angle over Jeddah, then widens into the shade that slides off the page. Under two seconds,
   skipped on return visits and under reduced motion.
2. **Live-shadow hero** — the concept line, set enormous, casts a shadow computed from the real solar azimuth and
   altitude at the restaurant right now. A live instrument line reads the hour: "Jeddah · 16:42 · sun 28° WSW —
   the long shade: coffee and sweets are being served." On desktop the pointer can hold the sun for a moment; at
   night the shadow comes from the courtyard lamp instead.
3. **A day in the courtyard** — a pinned, scrubbed chapter: scroll carries the sun across the house from dawn to
   midnight, the light warming, the lamps coming on, each hour's menu surfacing as it is served. Native scroll, never
   hijacked, with a skip link.
4. **The menu of the hour** — the menu opens on what is being served now; a sundial scrubber previews other hours;
   hovering a dish opens its photograph inside a tall arched window that trails the cursor and casts the hour's shadow.
5. **The dial gallery** — photographs arranged like hour marks on a dial; drag to turn it, with inertia; mirrored
   in RTL.
6. **Shade transitions** — a band of shade crosses the screen at the current shadow angle between pages. Optional
   ambient courtyard sound (off by default): fountain and birds by day, crickets and a distant oud by night.

## Direction B — **Milh · ملح**

**Cuisine & concept.** A Mediterranean fish-market house: whatever the boats bring at dawn, weighed at the counter,
grilled, fried or baked in salt, eaten with bread from the wood oven. Loud, social, generous.

**City.** Alexandria — Anfoushi, by the old fishing harbour.

**Positioning.** Casual, fast-turning, affordable, busy until late. The neighbourhood's long table.

**Story.** «بيننا عيشٌ وملح» — "there is bread and salt between us": the Arabic phrase for a bond you do not break
once you have eaten together. Milh is a room built for that bond.

**Concept line.** **"What is shared, binds."** — «ما نتقاسمه يربطنا»

**Palette.** Salt white, sea ink, sardine silver, tomato red, lemon yellow, harbour-rope beige.

**Type pairing.** Lalezar (Arabic display, hand-painted shop-sign weight) with Big Shoulders Display (Latin,
condensed market signage); IBM Plex Sans Arabic / Plex Sans for text.

**Photography.** Flash-lit, high-contrast market frames: ice beds, scales, hands with knives, bread fresh from the
oven, crowded tables from above.

**Motion language.** The tide: horizontal swells, liquid displacement, quick and a little bouncy.

**Signature moments.** A salt-pour preloader that fills the wordmark; a live "catch board" updated from the kitchen;
a tide-line hero that refracts the headline below the waterline (WebGL); a menu laid out on an ice bed where dishes
slide into place; a "bread and salt" share card that invites a friend to the table; harbour ambience on request.

## Direction C — **Mouneh · مونة**

**Cuisine & concept.** A Levantine pantry kitchen: summer's produce preserved for winter — makdous, kishk, labneh balls
in oil, apricot jam, pomegranate molasses, pickles — and the cooking that grows out of the jars.

**City.** Amman — Jabal Al-Weibdeh, an old limestone house with a vine over the door.

**Positioning.** Intimate, homely, mid-priced; a deli counter by day, a slow supper at night.

**Story.** Every Levantine grandmother kept a cellar of jars labelled in her own handwriting. Mouneh cooks from that
cellar, fills it again every season, and sells the jars by the door.

**Concept line.** **"Summer, kept for winter."** — «صيفٌ نخبّئه للشتاء»

**Palette.** Amber jar-light, apricot, olive-oil green, pomegranate, limestone white, kitchen-cabinet teal.

**Type pairing.** Aref Ruqaa (Arabic, the handwriting of jar labels) with Fraunces (Latin, soft and domestic); Noto
Naskh Arabic and Instrument Sans for text.

**Photography.** Back-lit glass, shelves, steam, flour, close crops of labels and hands sealing lids.

**Motion language.** Sealing and opening: lids turning, labels peeling back, jars filling from the bottom.

**Signature moments.** A jar-filling preloader; a pantry-shelf menu where every category is a shelf and every jar
glows when hovered; a seasonal calendar of what is being preserved this week; "open the jar" dish details; labels
written live in Ruqaa; a kitchen-radio ambience.

## The choice: **Zill**

1. **The concept is native to the medium.** A website can know where the sun is. Zill turns the brief's
   "time-aware atmosphere" from a feature into the idea itself: the site literally follows the sun over the
   restaurant, so every visit is different and true.
2. **One idea organises everything.** Menus (the hours), palette (light and shade), motion (moving shadows), content
   (a day in the courtyard), events (Zero-Shade Day in Jeddah), even Ramadan — where the most important moment of the
   day, iftar, is a sunset.
3. **Rooted, not clichéd.** Regional architecture expressed as light and shadow — no gold-on-black, no generic
   arabesque; the ornament is the shade itself.
4. **It scales across the whole brief.** A family breakfast and a seven-course tasting menu belong to the same idea, so
   the platform stays credible for casual and luxury operators alike.
5. **An ownable mark.** The Arabic letter ظ is already a sundial — a ground stroke, an upright gnomon and a dot for the
   sun. The monogram needed only to be drawn, not invented.
6. **Photography that unifies.** Hard light and long shadows are a direction any good photograph can be graded into,
   so sourced images can be made to look like one photographer's work.

Milh is lively but its tide language leans on familiar liquid effects; Mouneh is charming but its world is a
collection of objects rather than a system. Zill is a system — and a system is what a platform needs.
